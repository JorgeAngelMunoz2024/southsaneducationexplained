Where Is the Root-Cause Analysis?

Were Teachers Really What South San Needed to Fix? — Part 3

Published: September 2026

New to the series? Read Part 1 and Part 2.

In Part 2, we followed the public record through the April 14, 2025 vote to approve districtwide NIET/TAP. South San’s earlier Needs Assessment had not identified deficiencies in teaching practice as a districtwide root cause of low student performance, and a new Needs Assessment was still to come. But the story did not end with the April 14 vote.

What Happened After April 14?

What the Timeline Shows

The timeline picks up where Part 2 left off. On April 14, the Board approved districtwide NIET/TAP while the new Needs Assessment Saavedra had described was still to come. By August, South San’s Draft Implementation Plan listed “Administration Data Analysis & Root Cause Recommendations.”

Then, on August 25, the Board met for a workshop where Hinojosa and his team presented what was described as their “data analysis and root cause.”

Could this be the new Needs Assessment Saavedra had told the Board about in April?

On April 14, TEA Conservator Abe Saavedra had said he, Hinojosa, and district staff would conduct a new Needs Assessment over the following several weeks and present it to the Board. Yet districtwide NIET/TAP was approved that same night, before the new assessment was completed. As we saw in Part 2, the public materials for that vote did not include South San-specific data or analysis showing that deficiencies in teaching practice significantly contributed to low student performance.

Was the August presentation finally the analysis explaining why teacher practice had become such a major focus?

During the August 25 workshop, Hinojosa asked for a “PowerPoint presentation,” and participants repeatedly referred to and moved among slides while discussing the administration’s analysis.

But there is a problem: the presentation they were looking at doesn't appear in the public meeting video we reviewed. We can hear participants refer to and move between slides, but the viewer never sees the slides in the video. Read the video transcript here. We also didn't find the presentation among the publicly posted workshop materials.

So what was in the presentation the Board was looking at—and was it the Needs Assessment Saavedra said would later be presented?

In late August 2026, South San AFT—the local affiliate of the American Federation of Teachers—submitted a public-records request for “a copy of the PowerPoint on Data Analysis and Root Causes presented by Dr. Hinojosa at the August 25, 2025 Board of Managers special session.”

The district responded by sending the same two documents already publicly posted for the workshop—the Draft Implementation Plan and Corrective Action Plan/Exit Criteria—and stated: “There were no PowerPoints.”

What Did the Missing Presentation Contain?

By the August workshop, districtwide NIET/TAP had already been approved for four months. The administration was now presenting its data analysis and root-cause work, and one recommendation discussed was to “invest in teacher capacity.” The missing presentation could help show how the district moved from its data to that recommendation—and whether teacher-related factors were supported as a significant cause of low student performance.

Without the presentation, we cannot see what teacher-related evidence was shown, how strongly it supported the administration’s conclusions, or how those factors compared with other possible causes of low student performance.

The public record therefore leaves several questions unresolved. Why isn’t the presentation visible in the public workshop video? Why wasn’t it included with the posted workshop materials? And why, when South San AFT specifically requested the PowerPoint discussed during the meeting, did the district respond that there were no PowerPoints?

So We Asked the Board of Managers

The public record still did not tell us what additional South San-specific evidence, if any, individual Board members had reviewed before the April 14 vote. So on September 8, 2026, we sent questions individually to each member asking what role they personally played in reviewing the evidence, what South San-specific evidence they reviewed before approving districtwide NIET/TAP, whether the promised Needs Assessment or root-cause analysis was completed, and where the public can review that evidence.

As of September 24, 2026, no responses had been received. Check here to see whether any responses have been received since then. 

NIET/TAP was not a minor change. It involved additional costs, changed how teachers are trained, coached, observed, and evaluated, and shifted more instructional decision-making from individual teachers toward district-directed expectations and practices.

For a decision of that scale, the public should be able to review the underlying data and analysis—with personal or identifying information removed where necessary—so they can evaluate whether the evidence supports the district’s conclusions.

Were teachers really what South San needed to fix—and did the evidence support such a major districtwide restructuring of classroom instruction?

Based on the public record we reviewed, we still cannot answer that question. The August presentation may contain helpful information, but we cannot evaluate evidence we cannot see. Until the underlying analysis is available for public review—or additional evidence is provided—we cannot independently determine whether the evidence supported making deficiencies in teaching practice such a significant focus of South San’s improvement strategy.
