Was There Evidence That Poor Teaching Was a Significant Problem at South San?

Were Teachers Really What South San Needed to Fix? — Part 2

Published: September 2026

If you haven’t read Part 1, start here.

In an effort to improve student performance, South San adopted NIET/TAP, a model that puts changing teacher practice at the center of its improvement strategy. Teachers are trained, coached, observed, and evaluated against prescribed instructional practices. But before asking whether NIET/TAP was the right solution, there is a more basic question:

Was ineffective teaching actually a significant problem at South San?

To answer that, we started before Superintendent Hinojosa arrived and before TEA appointed the new Board of Managers. We looked at what South San itself had already identified as its major needs and challenges, then followed the public record forward to see what changed, what evidence was presented, and when.

South San NIET/TAP Timeline

What the Timeline Shows So Far

We started by looking for South San’s March 2024 Comprehensive Needs Assessment, but the district’s original PDF is no longer available at its former web address. Search engines still retain indexed text captured when the PDF was publicly available, even though the document itself is no longer online.
That indexed text shows that the assessment’s formal staff-quality concern was teacher turnover, not ineffective teaching. Other identified problems included learning loss, curriculum and intervention systems, data use, and related district processes.

Then came April 14, 2025. TEA Conservator Abe Saavedra told the Board that “over the next several weeks” he would conduct a new Needs Assessment with Hinojosa and district staff. He said the completed assessment would be presented to the Board and used to identify specific goals for the district. At that same meeting—before that assessment had been completed—Hinojosa recommended districtwide NIET/TAP, and the Board of Managers approved it 7–0.

That raised another question: Had the Board already considered districtwide NIET/TAP somewhere else? We reviewed the Board’s public meeting calendar and posted meeting records leading up to April 14. We found no public record showing earlier Board consideration of districtwide NIET/TAP.

South San had worked with NIET previously in a limited capacity, but that was much different from what was being proposed in April 2025.

So we looked at the April 14 vote itself. Hinojosa described NIET/TAP and asked whether Board members had any questions. No Board member asked a question about the proposal. After a motion and second, the chair again invited discussion or questions. None followed, and the Board approved districtwide NIET/TAP 7–0.

What South San-specific evidence had Board members reviewed before voting?

At this point, the public record gave us the sequence—but not the answer. The new Needs Assessment was still pending, we had found no earlier public Board consideration of districtwide NIET/TAP, and no Board member asked a question when it came up for approval. What we still had not found was the South San-specific evidence showing that problems with teaching were a major cause of low student performance.

So We Looked at What the Board Was Given

The official Board record includes an eight-page attachment for the April 14 NIET/TAP agenda item. It includes the agenda summary and NIET’s Statement of Work for districtwide TAP support at 12 schools, totaling $252,500. Under “Historical Data,” the summary states “none.” In the attachment, we found no South San-specific data showing how widespread problems with teaching were, no analysis connecting those problems to low student performance, and no root-cause analysis identifying teaching practice as a major cause of the district’s academic results.

So What Did the Board of Managers Review Before Voting?

The public record does not tell us what additional South San-specific evidence, if any, individual Board members reviewed before voting. So we asked them directly.

We Asked the Board of Managers

On September 8, 2026, we sent questions individually to each member of the Board of Managers asking what role they personally played in reviewing the evidence before the April 14 vote, what South San-specific evidence they reviewed before approving districtwide NIET/TAP, whether the promised Needs Assessment or root-cause analysis was completed, and where the public can review that evidence. As of September 25, 2026, no responses had been received. If a response is received, it will be posted here.

View Board responses and updates.

But the story did not end with the April 14 vote.

Four months later, the Board met for an August 25 workshop. This time, Hinojosa and his team presented what was described as their “data analysis and root cause.” During the discussion, Hinojosa asked for a “PowerPoint presentation,” and Board members referred to slides—including one about “building teacher capacity.”

Was this the analysis that finally showed why teaching practice had become such a major focus—and what did it actually show?
