Why Did South San Change How Teachers Teach?

Were Teachers Really What South San Needed to Fix? — Part 1

Published: September 2026

A Bit of Background: Why Did TEA Replace South San’s Board?

On February 19, 2025, TEA replaced South San’s elected Board of Trustees with a seven-member Board of Managers after years of documented governance problems, financial-control concerns, and repeated state investigations. TEA’s stated reasons for the intervention centered primarily on longstanding problems with district governance and management. 

Yet less than two months later, on April 14, 2025, the newly installed Board of Managers, acting on the superintendent’s recommendation, approved districtwide implementation of NIET/TAP, an outside-developed school-improvement model—before a new district Needs Assessment announced at that same meeting had been completed.

That decision affected both how teachers were expected to teach and how their teaching would be monitored and evaluated. Under NIET/TAP, teachers are coached, observed, and evaluated against a detailed instructional rubric. At the same time, South San’s broader curriculum requirements affected the materials teachers could use and how much latitude they had to adapt instruction to differences among students.

What Is NIET/TAP?

The model South San adopted was developed outside of the district. TAP (The System for Teacher and Student Advancement) was introduced in 1999 by Lowell Milken, whose professional background was in law, business, and philanthropy rather than classroom teaching. He later founded the National Institute for Excellence in Teaching (NIET).

At its core, NIET/TAP reflects a management-oriented theory of school improvement, using structured standards, professional development, coaching, observation, evaluation, accountability, and performance measures to improve teaching. Central to the model is the idea that improving teaching, as defined by the model’s instructional practices and expectations, will in turn improve student performance.

How is NIET/TAP changing teaching in South San classrooms?

In practice, NIET/TAP places a detailed, externally developed framework around decisions teachers would otherwise make more independently. Teachers—who bring subject expertise, professional training, experience, and day-to-day knowledge of their students—are expected to work within those prescribed practices and expectations, leaving less room to innovate, draw on their individual strengths, develop their own solutions to classroom problems, and find new ways to engage and inspire students.

What Does This Mean for Students?

Much of the real work of education happens in the moments when teachers use their knowledge, experience, creativity, and relationships with students to respond to what a class or an individual student needs. When teachers have less freedom to make those judgments, students may also lose some of the flexibility, creativity, individual attention, and responsiveness that can help them learn, stay engaged, and succeed.
